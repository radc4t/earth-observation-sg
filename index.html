Earth Observation · Singapore

Singapore, from space

Every day, satellites photograph our island in wavelengths the eye can’t see. This is what that data reveals — about our greenery, our heat, and the ships at our doorstep.

01 · Environmental monitoring

How green is Singapore, really?

Satellites measure how strongly the land reflects near-infrared light — healthy plants glow in it. Combined into a Vegetation Index (NDVI), it maps living green cover, from the dense canopy over the Central Catchment and Bukit Timah to the bare ground of a new worksite.

Who uses it
NParks, environmental agencies, researchers
Why it matters
Tracking green-cover change shows where the city is greening — and where tree cover is being lost — guiding reforestation and the “City in Nature” goal.

02 · Climate resilience

The city makes its own heat

Thermal sensors read the temperature of the ground itself. Industrial Jurong and Tuas, and dense built-up estates, store and re-radiate heat — an “urban heat island.” Reservoirs, the Botanic Gardens and the forested centre stay markedly cooler.

Who uses it
URA, HDB, climate-resilience planners
Why it matters
Seeing where heat concentrates tells planners where cooling matters most — shade, greenery, water and building design — as Singapore adapts to a warming climate.

03 · Maritime activity

One of the world’s busiest waterways

Ships broadcast their position over AIS, and satellites listen from orbit. Plotted together, the vessels trace the shipping lanes of the Singapore Strait — tankers, container ships and bulk carriers threading past the island in a near-constant stream. Tap a vessel to inspect it.

Who uses it
MPA, maritime security, port operations
Why it matters
Space-based vessel tracking supports safe navigation, pollution response and maritime security in one of the most heavily trafficked straits on Earth.

Earth Observation Initiative

Land, heat, and sea — one island, observed

Green cover, surface heat and maritime traffic are three views of the same place, each read from space. This is the promise of an Earth Observation Initiative: turning satellite data into understanding people can act on. Public agencies observe the Earth so the public can see it too.

About the data

Real imagery: the satellite basemap is genuine, free, public data — EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless (Copernicus Sentinel-2) and Esri World Imagery.

Real NDVI: the vegetation layer is genuine Sentinel-2 NDVI, computed from the free AWS Open Data Sentinel-2 L2A mirror (28 Jul 2024, clouds & water masked). The source bands are 10 m; the exported overlay is that source resampled to ~16 m/px for display — not a 10 m raster. See docs/swap-instructions.md.

Real surface temperature: the thermal layer is genuine Landsat 9 Collection-2 land-surface temperature in real °C (6 Jul 2025, cloud & shadow masked), from the keyless Microsoft Planetary Computer archive. Landsat's thermal band is 100 m, USGS-resampled to 30 m; displayed at ~32 m/px.

One illustration remains: the vessel layer shows simulated tracks, tagged in its legend — not live AIS. It is wired to accept a real AIS feed via a one-line swap.
